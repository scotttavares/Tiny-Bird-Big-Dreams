The Provenance Manifesto

A sanctuary for human craft. Zero algorithms. Zero AI. Every object traced back to a living maker.

Provenance is a marketplace built like a gallery and governed like a guild. No sponsored placement, no recommendation engine, no factory resale. Just work, the hands that made it, and the studio it came from.

  • No ranking algorithm
  • No pay-to-play ads
  • No generative AI art
  • No drop-shipping
  • No digital mockups

The Chronological Atelier Feed

Newest first. Always.

No hidden ranking, no boosted listings. A piece listed this morning by a first-year potter sits exactly where a thirty-year veteran’s would.

Sorted by: date listed. That’s the only sort there is.

Vetting Protocols

The door is open. It just isn’t automatic.

Open-door signups are how marketplaces fill up with resold factory goods and generated art. Provenance replaces them with a three-layer trust model reviewed by people.

i.

“Hands at Work” Application

Every applicant submits a portfolio plus a short video or photo series of raw materials, tools and their own hands mid-creation.

  • Wet clay on a wheel
  • Ink rolling onto a linocut block
  • Warp threads on the loom
ii.

The Authenticity Pledge

A binding seller agreement: zero generative AI, zero digital-art shortcuts presented as handwork, zero drop-shipping or third-party factory resale.

  • Breach means delisting
  • Buyers can flag any listing
  • Guild review, not a bot
iii.

Zero Digital Mockup Rule

Thumbnails are real photographs of the real object, in natural light or in the studio. No stock-generated living rooms, no rendered walls.

  • Photographed, not rendered
  • Process shots required
  • What you see is what ships

Platform Architecture

Built like a gallery, not a funnel.

The Atelier Stamp

An understated seal on every verified piece, naming the medium and the physical studio: Atelier: Portland, OR. Earned through vetting, never bought.

Story-First Pages

No countdown timers or “12 people have this in their cart.” Each listing reads like a studio visit: process documentation, notes on materials, and an Ask the Maker button.

Studio Fulfillment

Every maker ships from their own bench with discounted postage and processing timelines they set themselves. Heavy or fragile work can be marked for local studio pickup.

The Open Ledger

One flat commission. Nothing stacked on top.

Payments run through Stripe Connect. At checkout the sale splits instantly: the platform takes one flat commission, the processor takes its standard fee, and the rest goes straight to the maker’s bank account.

  • No listing fees
  • No forced “offsite ads” cut
  • No paid placement to buy back visibility
  • No surprise currency or subscription tiers

    Shop Migration

    Bring your shop. Leave the algorithm behind.

    Years of listings shouldn’t keep you stuck somewhere that stopped working for you. Connect your existing shop and your catalog comes across in minutes, ready for guild review rather than retyped by hand.

    1. Connect your shopSign in to Etsy, Shopify, Squarespace or Big Cartel, or upload the listings CSV your platform already lets you download. Imports use each platform’s official export or API. Nothing is scraped.
    2. Everything comes with youTitles, descriptions, photos, prices, variations, stock counts and shipping profiles are all mapped to Provenance fields and sorted into a medium.
    3. Guild check, not a rubber stampEvery import lands as a draft. Photos that look like digital mockups are flagged. Pieces that need process shots say so, and anything print-on-demand or factory-made is held back.
    4. Run both while you moveKeep your old shop open during the switch. Stock syncs both ways, so a piece that sells in one place comes off the other. Close the old shop whenever you’re ready.

    Comes with you

    • Listings, photos and prices
    • Variations and stock counts
    • Shipping profiles and processing times
    • Reviews, labelled with where they were earned, wherever the source platform allows it
    • Your shop story and About page

    Stays behind

    • Ad budgets and promoted listings
    • Search ranking games
    • Print-on-demand and third-party fulfillment
    • Stock-generated mockup photos
